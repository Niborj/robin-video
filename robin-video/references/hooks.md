# Hooks

The first two seconds decide whether anyone watches, and they are watched on mute. The hook is the first scene
(`archetype: hook`, or a `stat`/`statement` used as the opener). Write three options in `plan.md`, pick one, say why.

## Rules

- **The first frame is the hook.** The opener is a cut scene: its ring, logo and eyebrow are on screen from frame 1 and
  the headline starts rising immediately (readable by ~0.8 s). The delivered file opens on the poster (the settled
  hook) and dissolves into the live video over 0.3 s. No logo sting, no empty gradient, no slow fade from black.
- **3–9 words**, as large as the frame allows. `kit.py` fits hook and statement headlines automatically: one sentence
  per line when it stays large, otherwise balanced lines (up to three; four when that keeps every sentence starting on
  its own line), sized to the longest line (about 100–150 px in portrait, up to 136 px in square). Force your own breaks with `|`: `"Invisible text. | [The agent obeyed.]"`. Put the turn in `[brackets]`.
- **Outcome language.** Speak to the viewer's world, not the document's table of contents.
- **Specific beats clever.** A real number or a precise claim from the source outperforms wordplay.
- **Calmly confident.** Cato hooks create curiosity or recognition, never fear. "Your network is under attack" is off
  brand; "AI changed the attack surface. Security has to change with it." is on brand.
- **Pay it off.** The second beat delivers what the hook promised. Never bait.

## Formulas (with Cato-voice examples)

| Formula | Pattern | Example | Pairs with |
|---|---|---|---|
| The number | Lead with the most surprising figure | "[71%] of employees use AI tools IT never approved." | `stat` opener, count-up |
| The reframe | State a belief, then turn it | "More threats don't have to mean [more tools.]" | `hook`, highlight on the turn |
| Contrast pair | Two short sentences, old vs new | "AI moves fast. [Security has to see first.]" | `hook`, then `compare` |
| The question | A question your audience is already asking | "Do you know which AI apps your teams [used today?]" | `hook`, then `stat` answer |
| The tease | Promise a specific, countable payoff | "[Three moves] for safe AI adoption." | `hook`, then `list` |
| The finding | Research voice: what the data showed | "We analyzed 30 days of AI traffic. [Here's what we found.]" | `hook`, then `cards` |
| The myth | Name a common belief, then correct it | "Myth: blocking AI keeps you safe. [It just hides it.]" | `statement` pairs |
| The quote | A strong line from a real, cleared person | "“The policy conversation took [a day, not a quarter.]”" | `quote` opener |
| The moment | An event or launch, with stakes | "Booth #5120. [Agentic AI security, live.]" | `hook`, event story |

Cato's own tone-of-voice examples (Visual Style R101) show the register: "Security is complex. Securing it shouldn't
be." · "Complexity out. Clarity in." · "Stay ahead without chasing every threat." · "The old security playbook wasn't
written for AI." · "AI moves fast, Cato moves faster." Use them as a guide to rhythm, not as copy to reuse.

## Hook motion

The archetypes already animate hooks well (ring super-graphic settles, words rise masked, the highlight turns Highlight
Green as it lands). For the art-direction pass, add **one** bespoke move that makes this hook specific, for example:

- The number counts up inside a ring that draws to the same percentage.
- The turn word slides in late, after a 0.4 s beat of silence in the music.
- A real page from the document sits blurred behind the hook, then sharpens on the reveal.
- The contrast pair: the first sentence dims to grey as the second (highlighted) lands.

Keep it legible from frame 1: the poster (frame 0 of the delivered file) is usually the hook's settled frame.
